■PHY footprint scaling across HBM generations
• Conventional trend (HBM2→sHBM4): PHY footprint and CH length continuously expanded for higher BW.
• The inflection point (advanced logic): adopting advanced logic processes dramatically reduces PHY and 
D2D areas starting from HBM4 to HBM5.
■Impact of PHY shrinkage
• Benefit: shorter channel length significantly improves energy efficiency (lower pJ/b).
• Challenge: higher power density in smaller PHY areas creates thermal hotspots.

Summary and Conclusions
■Emergence of cHBM:
• Rapidly evolving AI xPU SoCs demand tailored memory architectures.
• Applying advanced logic processes to the HBM B-die unlocks unprecedented architectural flexibility.
■Three-step evolutionary roadmap:
• Phase #1 (offloading & optimization): reclaims xPU silicon area by adopting compact D2D PHYs and 
offloading memory controllers to the B-die.
• Phase #2 (functional expansion): integrates value-added logic—such as telemetry sensors, external 
memory expansion, and PEs—directly into the B-die.
• Phase #3 (true 3D convergence): eliminates 2D/2.5D interfaces via zHBM, achieving direct vertical 
integration of xPU logic and DRAM stacks for maximum energy efficiency.
